STERN · self-supervised video pretraining · summer 2026 internship

Pretraining a video encoder on shared lab workstations: one training failure, its cause, and what correcting it bought

STERN pretrains video encoders on unlabelled face clips and measures them on valence and arousal prediction. Training runs on lab workstations shared with teaching, with DiLoCo, a method in which machines train alone and merge rarely. For 230 rounds the loss fell while the encoder was losing information it had at initialisation. This page shows how that was found, what caused it, and what the corrected setting gives.

What worked

  • The cause was found and removed. DiLoCo's default outer step multiplies each update by up to 7. At 7× the residual-stream norm reaches 370 to 460 and a motion readout falls below its random-initialisation level. At 1× the norm stays near 90 and the readout stays near its random-initialisation level. That readout splits by token; a stricter one, split by clip, puts every DiLoCo setting below random initialisation (appendix B).
  • Encoders trained with the corrected setting carry signal downstream. On a frozen probe for valence and arousal, the from-scratch ViT-B reads 0.189 against 0.066 for a random initialisation; two public pretrained models read 0.200 and 0.209 on the same probe. A smaller from-scratch encoder, fine-tuned, beats a random initialisation by +0.031 (95% interval +0.005 to +0.057) at round 48; by round 78 the difference is no longer resolved.
  • Continued pretraining improves a released model. Fine-tuned CCC goes from 0.287 to 0.331 over 30 seeds; 29 of the 30 improve.
  • The training system. Decentralized training on a pool of 113 single-GPU workstations, with a watcher that keeps runs alive and keeps them off the machines during class hours.

What is still open

  • In the from-scratch ViT-B, blocks 4 to 12 still contribute almost nothing to the reconstruction. Ten changes to the recipe did not alter that. A 3-block encoder reaches the same loss, which points at the objective.
  • Fine-tuned, the from-scratch encoder is far from the released model: +0.03 over a random initialisation against about +0.26.

1 · DiLoCoTraining on machines that talk once per round

The pool is 113 lab workstations with one GPU each, joined by ordinary 1 GbE. Exchanging gradients at every step over that network is slower than training on one machine (appendix D). DiLoCo (Douillard et al., 2023) avoids the exchange: in each round every machine starts from the same weights and trains alone on its own data; the machines then send their weight changes, the changes are averaged, and an outer optimiser applies the average to the shared weights.

The outer optimiser is SGD with Nesterov momentum. With g the averaged update, b a buffer and w the shared weights:

b ← momentum · b + g w ← w − outer_lr · (g + momentum · b)

If g keeps the same direction, the step grows round after round towards outer_lr / (1 − momentum) times g. With the paper's defaults, 0.7 and 0.9, that is 7. The figure plays three rounds as you scroll.

Left: a schematic of three rounds, not data. Grey: each machine's own training. Blue arrow: the average of the updates. Orange arrow: what the outer step actually applies. Right: the formula above, for a constant update direction, with the five outer settings used in this project (outer learning rate / momentum). The value at the end of each curve is its limit; for a real run it is an upper bound.

2 · The symptomThe loss falls, and one block does the work

Every early run was ranked on reconstruction validation loss. This is the longest one, on 16 machines with the default outer step.

Why the next figure. A falling loss does not say which parts of the encoder are used. A block-skip test does: replace one block with the identity and measure how much the held-out reconstruction error rises.

Skipping block 1 costs 0.26 to 1.12. Skipping any one of blocks 4 to 12 costs at most 0.017. After 100 rounds on 16 machines block 2 contributes as well (block 1 costs 0.42, block 2 0.16), and blocks 4 to 12 still do not (at most 0.014).

3 · The causeThe size of the update

Define the effective multiplier as outer_lr / (1 − momentum) × lr_mult, and reach the same value by two routes: through the outer step, or with plain AdamW and a larger learning rate. Two measurements follow it. The first is the norm of the residual stream after each block. The second is motion R²: a linear map from the frozen last-block tokens to how much each patch changes between frames, which says whether the encoder keeps information about motion.

At 0.7× and 1× the norm ends near 90 and motion R² stays at 0.64 to 0.66, the level of a random initialisation (0.64). At 7× the norm ends between 370 and 460 and motion R² falls to 0.53 to 0.54 with the DiLoCo outer step, and to 0.41 to 0.43 with plain AdamW at seven times the learning rate.

Reading

Plain AdamW at 7× is affected more than DiLoCo at 7×. So the outer momentum is not what does it: the size of the update is. The validation loss cannot show it: across all these settings it stays between 0.513 and 0.548.

4 · The correctionTraining at 1×, and what the encoders are worth

Two downstream checks were run on encoders trained at an effective multiplier of 1×. Both use AFEW-VA (600 clips, 240 actors) and the concordance correlation coefficient (CCC) on held-out actors: 1 is perfect agreement with the human annotation of valence and arousal, 0 is none.

Frozen features

Why this figure. It asks whether the encoder's features carry valence and arousal without any fine-tuning: the encoder is frozen and a small attentive probe is trained on its layer-6 tokens.

The from-scratch ViT-B, trained for 60 rounds on 9 machines at 1×, reads 0.189 against 0.066 for its control. A public VideoMAE V2 ViT-B fine-tuned on UCF101 action labels reads 0.200 and the released LeVJEPA ViT-L 0.209 on the same probe. Scope: three seeds, one layer, a small probe; the controls are not seeded, so differences are read within a row.

Fine-tuned, against a random initialisation

Why this figure. Fine-tuning is the stricter test, because a random initialisation also learns when fine-tuned. The encoder and a random initialisation are fine-tuned with the same 10 seeds, and the paired difference is reported with a 95% interval.

At round 48 the encoder reaches a CCC of 0.089 against 0.058 for a random initialisation: +0.031, interval +0.005 to +0.057. At round 78 the difference is +0.011 and the interval includes zero. Earlier snapshots are shown without intervals because of a seeding bug fixed on 2026-11-01 (appendix D). For scale, the released ViT-L scores about +0.26 on this test. It is one training run.

5 · Still openThe deep blocks

Lowering the update removes the norm growth and the loss of motion information. It does not make blocks 4 to 12 contribute: in section 2 the blue curves are as flat as the orange ones. Ten variants of the recipe were tried against that, and none changes it (appendix A).

A 3-block encoder trained with the same recipe reaches the same validation loss as the 12-block one: 0.516 and 0.518 against 0.524 and 0.525, two seeds each. The reconstruction objective at this scale can be met with a few blocks, so the next thing to change is the objective, not the optimiser.

A stricter version of the motion readout, split by clip, shows the same thing from another side: motion information decreases with depth in every trained encoder (appendix B).

6 · Continued pretrainingImproving a released model on unlabelled face video

Start from the released LeVJEPA ViT-L, continue its self-supervised pretraining on the same unlabelled face video for about 1,080 encoder steps, then fine-tune on AFEW-VA. Thirty seeds per arm, one job per seed.

The gain is in valence, whose CCC goes from 0.090 to 0.176; arousal is unchanged (table in appendix E). Scope: 48 held-out actors, 102 clips, one identity split. The snapshot was chosen by a small frozen probe among five runs at four learning rates.

7 · The systemRunning on machines shared with teaching

Exchanging gradients at every step was measured first: eight machines together process 11.1 samples per second where one alone processes 24.0. So each machine trains alone and merges once per round. A watcher advances every run each minute, restarts a dead job once, and keeps every job off the machines on weekdays from 08:00 to 20:00 (appendix D).

8 · LimitsWhat these results do not cover

  • Sections 2 and 3 and appendices A and B rest on 20-round, single-machine encoders, mostly one seed per setting. The dose-response was pre-registered as a rank test (Spearman at most −0.8, p < 0.05). Four doses survive for the stricter readout, too few for that threshold: the test gives ρ = −0.80, p = 0.17.
  • The frozen probe of section 4 is three seeds on one layer. The ViT-B encoders have been through only an earlier 3-seed version of the paired fine-tuning test, which was inconclusive: −0.056 against −0.030 for random initialisation, 95% interval −0.277 to +0.225.
  • The fine-tuning result of section 4 is one training run, and the difference is no longer resolved at round 78.
  • All downstream numbers are on AFEW-VA.

B · A stricter motion readout

Why this figure. The motion readout of section 3 splits train and test by token, so tokens of one clip sit on both sides. This version splits by clip. It was run on a second batch of encoders, from 0.7× to 3×.

With this split, motion information is highest at block 1 and decreases with depth in every trained encoder, faster at a larger update: the last block reads 0.33 at 0.7×, 0.24 at 1×, and −0.13 at 3×. A random initialisation stays near 0.55. The 9-machine 1× encoder holds up best, from 0.65 at block 1 to 0.49 at block 12.

C · A second collapse: rank collapse in the small encoder, and its fix

The small LeVJEPA encoder had its own failure. With its regulariser (SIGReg) computed on batches of 16, the effective rank of its final token falls to about 1.2 within one round. Pooling the regulariser over a batch of 256 removes it.

With the pooled batch the rank reaches 10.4 and speaker identity 0.21, on both seeds, against 2.7 and 0.168 at a random initialisation. The rank fix alone did not improve valence and arousal: fine-tuned, these encoders scored about 0.03 below a random initialisation (measured before the seeding fix).

D · The system in more detail

  • One round. Assign shards (each held by exactly one machine), run local steps, wait for a quorum or a grace deadline, merge (drop stale and non-finite updates, weighted average, Nesterov outer step), sync the new master. The quorum shrinks for missing machines, never below 60% of the pool.
  • Watcher. A one-minute tick advances every run's state machine and runs six detectors: dead job, stalled job, foreign GPU process, quota, drained node, NaN. A dead job is resubmitted once; a second death escalates.
  • Class-hours gate. No job runs on weekdays from 08:00 to 20:00, and a job is refused if any part of its walltime would overlap that window. Enforced in the job scripts, in a shim in front of the scheduler commands, and in the watcher.
  • Seeding of the fine-tuning test. Until 2026-11-01 the test did not apply its seed, so seeds within one job could repeat and intervals computed before that date are too narrow. Only results re-run after the fix are given with an interval.
